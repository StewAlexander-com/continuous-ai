Local · Offline · Cross-Platform

Meet Aida — a truly honest local AI.

Aida is a fully offline assistant that won't make things up — and carries a reasoning memory you own, audit, and correct in plain language. No mainstream cloud assistant ships both together.

In a reproducible 5-run ablation, her integrity guards cut a small model's confabulation from 20% to 0%. She runs on Ollama, critiques her own answers, and grows from experience — no cloud, nothing leaves your machine. (Aida is the assistant; Continuous-AI is the runtime that powers her.)

What it does

Persistent, self-critiquing memory — entirely on your machine.

Most local LLM setups are amnesiacs: every chat starts from zero. Continuous-AI adds a versioned, machine-writable memory layer and a self-critique pass on top of Ollama.

Persistent memory

A Mutable Context Map stores reasoning preferences, frameworks, and confidence traces in LanceDB — reasoning state, not a chat log. She picks up exactly where you left off.

Teach it live

Say “Remember…” or “your name is…” and the fact is promoted to an always-injected persona layer, saved the instant you type it. Durable facts persist; tangents fade.

Correct it live

“That’s wrong — the correct X is Y” prunes the stale fact by your words and saves your verbatim correction, deterministically. The model never decides what to delete.

Honest by design

A capability-boundary guard tells the model it’s fully offline. Instead of pretending to read a URL, she says she can’t and asks you to paste the text — so confabulations never reach durable memory.

Beliefs earned through friction

Before a model-derived insight becomes durable, it must survive a thesis → antithesis → synthesis pass. Consensus is treated as suspect; the dissent is kept, not buried.

Learns your reasoning style

The reasoning posture injected into every prompt is folded from what survives critique — a conservative, deterministic moving average you can always ask “why” about. Measured: 0→67 framework invocations across 8 probes, no honesty regression.

Self-critique & graded caution

A second pass grades every reply for coherence, contradiction, and drift — off the reply path, zero added latency. When coherence slips, a downward-only controller raises restraint on her next reply; it can only ever add caution, and every decision is auditable.

A real voice — offline

A natural neural voice (Kokoro) generated entirely on your machine — no cloud TTS. What she speaks is always a verbatim subset of the printed reply, and she never voices code, paths, numbers, or file contents.

Also measured: the self-shaping cognition (L3) layer. Answering the skeptics

The critics are right about AI. That's the design brief.

The sharpest critiques of AI — from safety researchers to structural skeptics — aren't about model quality. They're about unaccountable power: systems that invent facts, flatter their users, can't be audited, and drift with no one watching. Aida treats each critique as a design requirement — the critique is the antithesis; the mechanism is the synthesis.

“It just makes things up.”

A capability-boundary guard tells the model, every session, that it's fully offline — so she says she can't reach a URL and asks you to paste it, rather than inventing its contents. Measured: ~20% → 0% confabulation on the same 3B model.

How it forms beliefs

The user owns truth. The model earns its beliefs.

Memory is split in two. Facts you state are authoritative and verbatim. The model’s own conclusions have to survive an argument before they’re kept — a small deliberation that preserves the disagreement instead of averaging it away.

Thesis

The candidate

A model-derived insight from the session, proposed as-is — not yet trusted just because one pass produced it.

Antithesis

The strongest objection

A voice whose only job is to find where the claim is false, overstated, or context-dependent — or to say plainly there’s no real objection.

Synthesis

The earned belief

A revised belief that accounts for the objection rather than burying it. The surviving dissent is stored alongside it.

Consensus is suspect
If nothing objects, that’s flagged as low-information — an explicit anti-echo-chamber bias — not celebrated.
Depth scales with disagreement
A weak objection earns one round; a strong one earns more — hard-capped so the model never stalemates and always answers.
Beliefs grow across threads
A surviving belief is injected into future sessions; re-deriving it reinforces it, while the weakest decay out.
It curates its own signal
A deterministic score grows as a belief is re-earned and decays with age and lost conflicts. Contradictions are settled by the same deliberation; the loser is archived, not deleted, and revives if re-earned.
Doubt must be real
The model may challenge its own reasoning — never a fact you stated about yourself. User-anchored truth bypasses the doubt machine entirely.
She asks you on the hard ones
On a genuinely difficult turn she pauses and asks you to co-author the belief rather than guess — kept rare by a model-free difficulty gate, and always about her reasoning, never a smuggled fact.

Honest scope: this is contradiction-driven belief revision, not “self-awareness,” and there’s no literal fractal geometry — those are inspiration, not mechanism. It runs only on the model’s own insights; your facts and corrections stay verbatim. All of it happens off the reply path, so the conversation stays responsive.

Architecture

Five subsystems, one continuous loop.

MCM
Mutable Context Map — persistent, versioned, AI-writable state across threads, including the earned-belief layer.
TCB
Thread Continuity Bridge — loads state in at start, extracts a delta at end.
CRITIC
Internal Observer — scores coherence, contradiction, and drift, graded in the background.
DELIB
Deliberation — thesis → antithesis → synthesis over model insights; doubt bounded to never touch user facts.
RDST
Regressive Dynamic Self-Tuning — recency-weighted, gated LoRA adaptation.
  start ─► MCM.restore_context() ─► inject persona + earned beliefs ─► Ollama chat
                                                              │
                                  reply (streamed) ◄──────────┘
                                     │
         ┌───────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────┐
         ▼ (background, off the reply path)                          ▼
  CRITIC.evaluate() ─► coherence / drift         DELIBERATE() ─► thesis/antithesis/synthesis
                                     │
  end ─► delta extraction ─► MCM.write_delta() + promote_belief() + L3 consolidation() ─► LanceDB + snapshot
                                     │
       (after N threads) ─► RDST.score_threads() ─► [approval] ─► LoRA tune

Quickstart

Clone and run.

Runs on macOS (Apple Silicon — the primary, best-tested target), Linux, or Windows. Needs Python 3.11–3.13 and Ollama; the setup script handles the rest. (Optional LoRA self-tuning is Apple-Silicon-only.)

$ git clone https://github.com/StewAlexander-com/continuous-ai.git $ cd continuous-ai $ bash setup.sh # one-time: venv, deps, pull the model $ bash run.sh # starts Ollama if needed, then chat

Prefer a button? On macOS, double-click Seedling.command. Other commands: bash run.sh status, bash run.sh eval, bash run.sh fresh.

Verify it yourself

One command exercises the whole stack.

A single end-to-end smoke test runs against your live local model in an isolated, throwaway database — so it never touches your real memory — and prints a pass/fail line for each step.

$ bash run.sh smoke # end-to-end, live model, isolated temp DB
Start & restore
Loads prior context and injects it into the session.
Streaming + background critic
Reply streams token-by-token; grading runs off the reply path.
Teach & correct live
A “Remember…” directive is saved; a correction prunes and replaces the right fact.
Deliberation & belief growth
Session end forms at least one earned, deliberated belief.
Autonomous belief calculus
A contradiction is flagged (not merged) and resolved; a low-signal belief is quarantined, then revives when re-earned.
Cross-thread persistence
Persona facts, earned beliefs, and the archive all survive a fresh reload.

17 checks across 7 sections. The test is in the repo (smoke_test.py); add --model NAME to run it against a different local model. Honest scope: a functional smoke test that proves the pipeline works end-to-end on a real model and machine — not a performance benchmark. Results depend on your hardware and model.
